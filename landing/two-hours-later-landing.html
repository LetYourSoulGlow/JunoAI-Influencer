You know the moment

You typed "sure!" and a very small voice said no.

A colleague asks you to cover Saturday. You had plans, and you mention none of them. A friend makes a joke at your expense in front of everyone. Everyone laughs. You laugh too.

Then, two hours later, in the shower or on the bus, the perfect reply arrives. Complete. Calm. Devastatingly polite. So you decide you lack something: wit, confidence, assertiveness.

The shower reply is not the wit you were missing. It is what you sound like when nobody is watching.

In a live exchange your mind is doing two jobs: answering the question, and managing the face in front of you. The second job is faster, louder and older. Two Hours Later is about that second job, and how to stop it answering for you.

Chapter 1 · The reply that arrives two hours later

The Two-Hour Test

When a request or a comment lands, and you feel the familiar scramble, ask:

What would I say if nobody's reaction mattered?

Not what you would do. Only what you would say. You can still be kind, still be flexible, still say yes. But you will be choosing it, instead of tumbling into it.

Most people discover the answer is short

I can't do Saturday.
I didn't love that.
I'd like to go home.

Pause and write

  • Think of a recent moment when the right words came to you later. What was the request or comment?
  • What was the shower version? Write it exactly as it arrived.
  • What did you say instead, and who was in the room, or on the screen?

The part Juno would keep

The Two-Hour Map: six questions, about ten minutes

For after any hard exchange, the one that leaves you flustered, guilty or replaying. Take the questions in order.

  1. What actually happened? What a camera would have recorded. Facts, not meanings.
  2. What did I know before I started doubting? Your first, quiet answer, before the sigh.
  3. What did they do when I said it? Their response, not their character.
  4. Whose feeling am I carrying? Harm is mine to repair. Their disappointment is theirs.
  5. What do I want to do now? Leave it, hold it, go back or repair. All four are legitimate.
  6. What is the smallest honest sentence? Three sentences or fewer. Read it aloud.
Ideas you'll keep using

Small enough to remember mid-conversation

The yes tax

Every yes you didn't mean gets paid later, in time, energy, resentment or cancellations. A kind no today is cheaper than a late cancellation next week.

The pause sentence

"Let me check and get back to you." You don't have to decide at the speed of the question. It's a pause with manners.

Guilt is not evidence

Guilt is a smoke alarm: sensitive, loud and very good at its job. It tells you someone is unhappy. It isn't a verdict on you.

The three-sentence rule

If your reply runs past three sentences, cut the one that is a justification. A reason is a courtesy, not a legal defence.

What it is

  • A slow look at the moment between the question and your answer.

What it isn't
